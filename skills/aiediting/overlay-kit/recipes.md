# Recipes: show it, do not write it

For the executors of scenes (the brief in `brief-template.md` points here) and for whoever plans a video. A scene exists so that the viewer **sees and understands**, not so that they read what they already hear. Times are for 24–30 frames a second.

## Three levels

1. Text on the screen: the weakest. Allowed only for the one key thought (one to three words, large) and for a name that must be seen written.
2. Real content: a video, a photo, a screenshot of the actual thing.
3. **Motion that shows the subject of the speech and what happens to it**: the strongest. A change is always a process: before → action → after.

Speech about a gallery shows a real gallery with the person's own photos, not a plate with the word «Галерея». «Two terabytes became a hundred gigabytes» is not the text «2 ТБ → 100 ГБ» but a gallery that is cleaned before the eyes with a counter melting down. A story is a series with progress bars, never one picture. A timeline is a live one that assembles itself from layers. A plate «icon + the very words being said» is empty: do not make it.

## Recipes (pick by the subject, alternate the kind)

Full-screen, the speaker's voice goes on:
- **a counter melts**: a real interface, a red figure, junk flies into a bin in two or three passes, the figure turns green (6–12 s);
- **chaos becomes order**: hundreds of thumbnails scatter into labelled albums that settle into a grid (5–10 s);
- **a search that finds**: the line types a word, «0 results» first, then the right items surface as a grid (5–8 s);
- **a card that describes**: a frame large, beside it lines print one by one: what, where, when, who (4–6 s);
- **a person found in a crowd**: three photos, a highlight sweeps a gallery and a frame lands on the face in a group (5–7 s);
- **a timeline assembles**, or **falls into layers** in 3D, one line to a layer (4–8 s);
- **before / after**: a grey talking head, a wipe, the same shot with everything added (4–6 s);
- **a chat window**: the request typed, the answer as a checklist whose marks come on the words (5–8 s);
- **folders create themselves**, **a calendar fills**, **two scales** (to generate costs; to shoot weighs more) for the matching lines (3–6 s).

A phone with a real interface: a **story series** (bars on top, a new one every second or so), a **profile** with its grid scrolling, a **reel with a counter**, **messages arriving**, an **export** unpacking into years.

Beside the speaker (a transparent layer in the free part of the frame, never on the face):
- **a word on the wall**: 1–3 words printed, struck out or dropped on the key word (2–4 s);
- **a huge word behind the head**, the person cut out over it: one per chapter at most (2–4 s);
- **a glass list**: only a real list, items on their words, as long as the listing lasts;
- **a card with their living video**, no caption; **a triptych** of three such panels for a list of hobbies;
- **a figure** from beyond the edge with one word, only measured and true (2–3 s); **tool icons** in a row joined by a thin line.

Titles and links: a **chapter title** (number from beyond the edge, the name, a thin scale over all chapters, 2–3 s); a **map of the video** (all chapters, the current lit) at the start; an **intro montage** of their own footage in even panels (8–12 s).

## The rules that make it read as expensive

- **Alternate the formats.** The same device twice in a row is dull: full-screen, then beside the speaker, then a phone, then a word on the wall. Transparent layers go on every other angle.
- **The person does not vanish.** Full-screen scenes one after another for more than about 15 s: a small window with the speaker's camera, rounded corners, in the lower right corner. Shorter than 10–15 s needs none, and neither does the opening. The window never covers text, a folder or what the speech is about: when something important is in that corner, the window leaves for that time.
- **No two identical screens.** Never the frame of this video's camera inside a scene while the window shows the same person: take the person's other videos for such a frame.
- **Events on the frame of the word.** Each appearance stands on the frame of the word that names it, or two or three frames earlier; the length of a scene is exactly the phrase; it leaves when the thought is over.
- **Away past the edge.** Things enter from beyond the frame with a soft landing (10–18 frames, ease-out) and leave faster (8–12 frames, ease-in) beyond the frame, never dissolving in place. 4–5 frames between the words of one line. Motion blur on what moves fast.
- **On a side angle the graphic stands in front of the face**, on the side the person looks to, never behind the head; a layer drawn for the main angle is shifted into the free part.
- **Air and a grid.** 30–40 % of the frame stays empty; panels of one size with one gap and one radius; real content inside, no placeholders; one accent colour; vector icons, no emoji.
- **Do not caption** a video or a story: it is clear on its own. A plate that only repeats the speech is cut.
- **The opening that works:** the person with a barely noticeable push in → a short full-screen scene of what the video holds → two seconds of the person (a side angle is best) → the scenes begin, short at first, no window yet.

## Planning

A table first: time, phrase, format, what is on the screen, where the content comes from. One idea per row, a scene about every 8–15 s, scenes never overlap, the person in the frame at least 40 % of a chapter. The first two or three minutes go to perfection and to the person's look at them before the rest is made in the same style. A drawn layout on paper does not show how it plays: put the draft in front of them.

## Before a scene is accepted

Stills of the scene on a real frame of the video as one sheet; then frames of the finished draft at several points of each scene. Each insert shows the subject and text only the key thought; the formats alternate; no emoji; everything leaves past the edge; the face is open, also when the speaker spreads their arms; events sit on the words; joints with cuts are exact or 3 s away; nothing the person asked not to show.
